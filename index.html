OpenFilings

Public federal filings, compiled into dated data files

We compile two things: federal prime contracts and orders whose period of performance ends inside the coming year, cut by six-digit industry code; and private foundations that filed a 990-PF return, cut by state, with their grants and officers.

Sources: the published federal award archive (archive 2026-08-06) and the federal 990-PF e-file XML archives. Both public domain. Snapshot 2026-09-07. Every file is a one-time purchase — no subscription, no login.

Federal contracts

Expiring federal contracts and orders

One six-digit industry code per file. Snapshot 2026-09-07, source archive 2026-08-06.

  • 30 columns: award id, vehicle id and type, incumbent, agency, office, values, dates, set-aside, PSC, award class, how to compete, official source link.
  • Three CSVs and one spreadsheet with typed numbers and dates, identical content; a free 20-row sample spread across the main sheets.
  • Every row links to its official award page; a 25-row audit matched at 96% or better.

Honest caveat: covers prime contracts and orders with activity in the current fiscal year; earlier-vintage industry codes are mapped to their 2022 successor; no subawards, no contacts.

  • S$29100–499 biddable-window rows · 181 codes
  • M$49500–2,499 biddable-window rows · 68 codes
  • L$792,500 or more biddable-window rows · 13 codes
Disclosures (8)
  1. Price keys on the biddable-window rows only. Imminent rows, 0–30 days to end, are included free and are likely too late to bid. Each listing states both counts separately.
  2. Lead time per file: the share of main rows with 90 or more days left runs 0.0632 to 1.0000, median 0.8000 across the 262 listed units; each listing states its own share.
  3. Orders outnumber standalone contracts: 114,853 of 188,740 main rows are orders under a vehicle, 60.9%, biddable only through that vehicle. The award_class and how_to_compete columns state it per row.
  4. Rows whose set-aside is sole source or a programme set-aside are biddable only by a firm holding that status; how_to_compete carries the qualifying phrase.
  5. Snapshot age: built 2026-09-07 from archive 2026-08-06. A file is rebuilt before upload if its snapshot is over 30 days old on the listing date.
  6. Crosswalk: a row filed under a pre-2022 industry code is published under its 2022 successor; naics_source_code keeps the code as recorded, and a code with several successors appears in each successor's file.
  7. Place of performance: pop_state is the 2-letter state of performance; blank means performance outside the United States. No mailing address, contact name, phone or email is included. Main rows whose potential end date is after the current end date may be extended rather than recompeted; each README carries that count.
  8. Compiled from public-domain federal data. No federal agency produced, reviewed, approved or endorses these files, and no agency trademark, logo or seal is used.

Foundation funders

Private foundation funders, grants and officers

One state per file. Snapshot 2026-09-07, e-filed 990-PF returns from 15 posted archives.

  • Per funder: assets, revenue, grants paid, contributions, application procedure text where filed, tax period, return id.
  • Grants itemized as filed: recipient, city, state, purpose, amount; placeholder rows counted in the README.
  • CSV and spreadsheet, identical content, plus free 20-row samples of funders and of grants.

Honest caveat: e-filed 990-PF returns only, posted in the 15 named archives. Foundations that filed on paper, and e-filers whose return was posted outside that window, are absent. No count of the absent filers is claimed.

  • S$2950–499 funders with grants · 15 states
  • M$49500–2,499 funders with grants · 26 states
  • L$792,500 or more funders with grants · 10 states
Disclosures (10)
  1. Coverage: e-filed 990-PF returns only, posted in the 15 archives from 2025_TEOS_XML_09A to 2026_TEOS_XML_07A. Paper filers and returns posted outside that window are absent; no count of the absent filers is claimed.
  2. Approachability: across the 51 listed states 25,674 of 111,619 funders (23.0%) filed Part XV application text and 81,059 (72.6%) state they give only to preselected organisations. Each listing prints its own state's counts.
  3. Tax period: one return per foundation, the newest filed. Newest tax periods run 2026-01-31 to 2026-07-31 across listed states; periods in the files run 2022-12-31 to 2026-07-31, 99.7% within 24 months of the snapshot.
  4. Mailing state: state is the address the foundation printed on its return, not where it gives. shared_admin_address = Y marks a row at an address shared by 50 or more of that state's funders — a trust company or administrator whose foundations may fund elsewhere: 17,974 funder rows.
  5. Part XV free text is cut at the e-file schema character limit where the filer wrote past it, so a long entry ends mid-word.
  6. Placeholder recipients: 3,045 of 1,455,674 grant rows (0.21%, worst state 0.42%) name an attachment, a bucket or a number instead of a recipient, because the filer wrote it that way. Each README prints its state's count.
  7. Dissolved foundations: final_return = Y where the filer marked the return as its last, 2,739 of 111,619 funder rows (2.45%).
  8. Contacts: only the phone, website and Part XV application contact the foundation itself printed on the return. No email column, no officer addresses, no appended or enriched contacts.
  9. Compiled from public-domain federal information returns. No federal agency produced, reviewed, approved or endorses these files, and no agency trademark, logo or seal is used.
  10. A free public roster of a state's registered private foundations exists and carries name, address, assets and income; what these files add is grants paid, officers and Part XV application text.

Verify any row

Nothing here has to be taken on trust. Every row carries the identifier that resolves it on the official source page.

Federal contracts
Every row links to its official award page in the source_url column; a 25-row audit against the official pages matched at 96% or better. End dates change when agencies modify contracts, so a row is accurate as of the archive date stated on the file.
Foundation funders
Every EIN is in the tax authority's e-file index, and each row carries tax_period_end and source_archive. A 25-row audit against the source XML matched at 96% or better, and a 10-funder check against the public record matched at 90% or better.

Questions

A failed link or a zip that will not open gets a replacement link, then a full refund on request. If the row counts, snapshot date or archive date on the listing differ from the delivered file, that is a full refund with no return needed. Refund window: 14 days from purchase.

An end date on the official page differs from the file.

No refund. The file reproduces the published federal archive as of the archive date printed on it, and every row links to its official page so the current date can be read there. Agencies modify contracts after the archive is published.
