How we verify a row

Nothing here has to be taken on trust. Every contract row links to its official award page; foundation rows cite the filing's EIN and tax period, which locate the public return. The audits are numbers, not adjectives.

Audit results measured 2026-09-07 on the shipped 2026-09-07 build of both file sets.

One rule: a row you cannot check is a row we should not sell

Both of our datasets are compiled from filings that anyone can read. So every row we publish carries the link or the identifiers that take you back to the filing it came from, and we test that path on a random sample before a file ships.

The link or identifier per row

Each federal contract row carries a link column pointing at that award's official page, plus the award id, the incumbent, the agency and office, both end dates and the industry code as recorded. Rows drawn at random from the build are fetched from the official award interface and compared field by field. On the 2026-09-07 build, 93 of 100 sampled rows matched the official award record on all 12 mapped fields (95% confidence interval 86%–97%); the 7 differences are later agency modifications, not mapping errors.

Each foundation row carries its EIN, the tax period and the return id, plus the name of the archive posting the return came from; the EIN and tax period are what locate the public return. The rows are then independently re-parsed from the source filings: 125 sampled rows across 25 state files — 100 grants and 25 funders — matched the filing on every audited column, audited 2026-09-07.

Snapshot dates, and what ageing does

A file is a photograph, not a feed. Every row carries the snapshot date, and the source archive it was built from is named on the file and on the listing.

Contract rows age visibly. Thirty days after the snapshot no row in the main sheets has expired, because the window starts at 31 days. Sixty days after it, 26,579 of 188,757 rows (14.1%) have passed their end date. That is why the snapshot and archive dates are printed on the listing and why a file older than a month gets rebuilt before it is offered.

End dates also move. Agencies modify contracts after an archive is published, so a row is accurate as of its archive date and the official page is the current answer. The link on the row is how you get to it.

What we do not do

  • We do not append contacts. The only phone, website or application contact in a foundation row is one the foundation printed on its own return. No email column, no officer addresses, no purchased or enriched contact data.
  • We do not correct a filer's numbers. If a foundation reported a figure oddly, the file reproduces it and the reconciliation count tells you how often the lines and the total disagree.
  • We do not include subawards, agency forecasts, or predictions about which contracts will be recompeted.
  • We do not score, rank or match rows to your capabilities.
  • We do not run alerts or subscriptions. A refresh is a new dated file with a new snapshot date.
  • We claim no endorsement. The sources are public-domain federal filings; no agency produced, reviewed or approved these files, and no agency trademark, logo or seal is used.

Where a number is unknown, it stays unknown. We publish no estimate of how many paper-filing foundations are missing, because we have not measured it.
