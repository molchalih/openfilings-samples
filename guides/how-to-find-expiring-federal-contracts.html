How to find federal contracts expiring in your NAICS code

Contracts do not appear on a list of things about to be recompeted. Here is what the free path actually returns, measured, and where it stops.

Every figure below was measured on 2026-09-07 against the published federal award archive dated 2026-08-06, or over the files we build from it.

The official search cannot filter on an end date

The public award search takes a date filter, but the date types it accepts are the action date, the date signed, the last-modified date, and a new-awards-only flag. The period-of-performance end date is not among them. Asked for one on 2026-09-07, the endpoint answers:

Field 'filters|time_period|date_type' is outside valid values 'action_date', 'date_signed', 'last_modified_date' or 'new_awards_only'

The bulk-download endpoint does not close the gap. On the same date a three-year request returned Invalid Parameter: date_range total days must be within a year, and the industry-code filter was dropped from the request it echoed back. So the site cannot hand you the slice you want, and an export of it is not available at any setting we could find.

What the bulk archive contains

The same programme publishes a monthly archive: one Full and one Delta file per month, plain download, no key and no account. Measured on 2026-09-07, the current-fiscal-year Full archive was 1,225,215,774 bytes zipped, five CSV members, 297 columns. It carries both end-date fields, the industry code, the awarding agency and office, the incumbent, the current and potential values, the set-aside and the place of performance.

That is the honest free path: download 1.2 GB, read 297 columns, keep the transaction that is current for each award, and filter locally. Across a 12-month window this yields 257,787 expiring awards spread over 935 industry codes; 273 codes hold 100 or more.

How to read the end dates

FieldWhat it isWhat to do with it
Current end dateThe date the work stops unless the agency actsThis is the recompete date. Count days from the snapshot date, not from today
Potential end dateThe end if every option is exercisedLater than the current end on 40,447 of 188,757 rows we shipped in the biddable window, 21.4%. Those may be extended rather than recompeted
Action dateThe date of the last modificationNot an end date. It is the field the official search actually filters on, which is why the search cannot answer this question

Standalone contracts and orders are not the same list

Of 188,757 rows in our biddable window, 114,864 are orders placed under an existing vehicle: 60.9%. A firm that does not hold the vehicle cannot bid those, whatever the end date says. Filter on the award class before you count your pipeline. The separate guide on orders and standalone contracts sets out the split.

What lead time is realistic

Working from a monthly archive, a quarter of what expires is already out of reach. Across 254,111 rows in a 395-day window, 65,371 end within 30 days, 25.7%. Of the rows past that point, 141,323 have 90 or more days left (74.9%) and 82,275 have 180 or more (43.6%); the median is 160 days.

Two further cuts matter before a row becomes a target. Among 73,893 standalone rows, 2,847 (3.85%) carry a sole-source or programme set-aside and can be won only by a firm holding that status. And 17,971 rows in the window (9.5%) were filed under a pre-2022 industry code, so a code-by-code search that ignores the crosswalk misses them.
